Most people adjust their seat and mirrors but never touch the headrest. It's worth a minute: the head restraint is there to limit how far your head snaps backward in a rear-end collision, the kind of motion associated with whiplash.

## The two checks

**1. Height.** Raise the headrest so the top is about level with the top of your head. If it can't go that high, set it as high as it goes. A headrest set too low can act as a pivot point rather than support.

**2. Distance.** Bring it as close to the back of your head as is comfortable. Many seats let you tilt the headrest forward or adjust your seatback angle to close the gap.

## Quick habits

- **Re-check after anyone else drives your car.** Headrests get bumped and lowered.
- **Sit upright.** A steeply reclined seatback pulls your head away from the restraint.
- **Check the back seat too.** Rear headrests are often left in their lowest position.

## If you've already been in a crash

Neck stiffness, headaches, and shoulder pain can show up hours or days after a collision. In Florida, PIP generally requires initial care within 14 days of the accident for benefits to apply. If you have warning signs like numbness, weakness, or a severe headache, go to the emergency room first.
