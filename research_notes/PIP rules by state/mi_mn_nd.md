## 1. PIP / basic economic-loss medical limits, coverage options, fee schedules, chiropractic coverage

### Takeaway
- **Michigan** (policies issued or renewed after July 1, 2020) has selectable PIP medical tiers: unlimited, $500k, $250k, $250k with exclusions, $50k for Medicaid enrollees, and opt-out with qualified health coverage. Assigned-claims cases are capped at $250k. Providers are paid on a Medicare-based fee schedule (MCL 500.3157). For most providers that schedule stepped down from 200% to 195% to 190% of Medicare, and **190% has applied since July 2, 2023**. Family attendant care is capped at 56 hours a week. In 2020, PA 104 removed the old "Jan 1, 2009 scope of practice" limit on chiropractic coverage, effective for services on or after July 2, 2021.
- **Minnesota** has $40k basic economic loss: $20k medical and $20k non-medical. Chiropractic is paid from the medical pool.
- **North Dakota** has $30k total basic no-fault benefits per person per accident. It is a single aggregate pool for all economic loss, not a separate medical pool.

### Cited Findings

**Michigan: PIP medical options (MCL 500.3107c, 500.3107d)**
- For policies issued or renewed after July 1, 2020, the options include $50,000 (Medicaid enrollees), $250,000, $500,000 and unlimited — [Michigan Auto Law, PIP coverage levels](https://www.michiganautolaw.com/no-fault/no-fault-act/pip-medical-benefits-coverage/)
- Since July 1, 2020, drivers must choose one of **six** PIP medical coverage levels — [1-800-LeeFree 2026 guide](https://www.1800leefree.com/blog/michigan-car-accident-lawyer-guide)
- The reform replaced mandatory unlimited PIP with tiers, let drivers opt out of medical PIP entirely, capped family attendant-care payments, and imposed a Medicare-anchored fee schedule — [Chahal, "2019 Reform Five Years Later"](https://1844mchahal.com/michigan-no-fault-2019-reform-explained/)
- Bulletin 2020-33-INS repeats that people with "qualified health coverage" may choose to limit PIP — [autonofaultlaw.com bulletin summaries](https://autonofaultlaw.com/insurance-bulletin-summaries/)
- Claimants who go through the Michigan Assigned Claims Plan have medical coverage capped at $250,000 — [Michigan Auto Law](https://www.michiganautolaw.com/no-fault/no-fault-act/pip-medical-benefits-coverage/)
- When PIP is exhausted, the injured person may sue the at-fault driver for excess economic damages. Health insurance, Medicaid or Medicare may pick up the bills — [Michigan Auto Law](https://www.michiganautolaw.com/no-fault/no-fault-act/pip-medical-benefits-coverage/)

**Michigan: fee schedule (MCL 500.3157)**
- **Main schedule, MCL 500.3157(2) (most providers, chiropractors included):**
  - 200% of Medicare for services from July 2, 2021 to July 1, 2022
  - 195% from July 2, 2022 to July 1, 2023
  - **190% from July 2, 2023 onward**
  - Sources: [MCL 500.3157](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-500-3157) (search extract); [Michigan Auto Law, Oct 8, 2025](https://www.michiganautolaw.com/blog/2025/10/08/michigan-no-fault-medical-fee-schedule/) (lists 190% as current); [acbilling.com, Chiropractic Billing in Michigan](https://acbilling.com/chiropractic-billing-michigan/) (190% is the current rate for chiropractors' Medicare-priced services)
- **Higher tiers for some providers:** other statutory tiers (about 220%–230%) apply to other provider categories, such as certain hospitals and trauma centers. A 250% rate applies to providers that deliver a large share of certain treatment — [MCL 500.3157](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-500-3157) (search extract). These were **not confirmed to apply to chiropractors**.
- **Services with no Medicare amount:** payment is a percentage of the provider's January 1, 2019 "charge description master" (chargemaster) rate, falling to **52.5% from July 1, 2023** — [acbilling.com](https://acbilling.com/chiropractic-billing-michigan/)
- **CPI adjustment:** Bulletin 2023-13-INS raised the Jan 2019 chargemaster-based amounts by **9.66%** for dates of service from July 2, 2023 to July 1, 2024. The statute requires an annual CPI update — [DIFS Section 271 Report 2023](https://www.michigan.gov/autoinsurance/-/media/Project/Websites/autoinsurance/PDFs/AutoInsurance/Section_271_Report_2023.pdf)
- Items not furnished by providers (home modifications, non-prescription drugs, non-emergency transportation) are outside the fee schedule. They are judged by the reasonableness standard of MCL 500.3107(1)(a) — [michiganlegalcenter.com on MCL 500.3157](https://www.michiganlegalcenter.com/mcl-§-500-3157-medical-fee-schedules-for-pip-charges-post-2019-reform-2)
- The most recent DIFS billing guidance found is **Bulletin 2025-11-INS**, "Payment and Billing Guidance for No-Fault Automobile Insurers and Health Care Providers." It supersedes Bulletin 2024-06-INS — [autonofaultlaw.com bulletin summaries](https://autonofaultlaw.com/insurance-bulletin-summaries/)
- The Medicare-based schedule caps hourly attendant-care rates for care "rendered after July 1, 2021" — [Michigan Auto Law, June 4, 2026](https://www.michiganautolaw.com/blog/2026/06/04/hourly-rate-for-attendant-care/)

**Michigan: attendant care**
- In-home attendant care is capped at **56 hours per week** when provided by family or household members. The cap does not apply to care from a nurse employed by a commercial agency or care in a residential facility — [Michigan Auto Law, June 2026](https://www.michiganautolaw.com/blog/2026/06/04/hourly-rate-for-attendant-care/)

**Michigan: chiropractic coverage (MCL 500.3107b)**
- **The old rule.** MCL 500.3107b(b) barred PIP recovery for chiropractic services unless the service "was included in the definition of practice of chiropractic under section 16401 of the public health code ... as of January 1, 2009" — [Warner Norcross + Judd](https://wnj.com/updates/coa-reimbursement-for-chiropractic-services-not-required-under-michigan-no-fault-act-as-services-did-not-fall-under-2009-definition-of-practice-of-chiropractic-service); [Secrest Wardle 2016](https://secrestwardle.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/No-Fault-Newsline-021616.pdf)
- **What the rule excluded:**
  - Services added by 2009 PA 223 (effective Jan 5, 2010), such as care beyond the spine, extremity adjustment and "physical measures," were excluded. Examples cited: massage, heat or cold therapy, traction, electrical stimulation, ultrasound, and exams or therapy beyond the spine.
  - *Measel v Auto Club* (Mich. Ct. App. 2016) denied payment for a new-patient exam, ultrasound and massage.
  - Sources: [Plunkett Cooney](https://www.plunkettcooney.com/transportation-law-hub/chiropractic-coverage-michigan-No-Fault); [COA opinion, Feb 9, 2016](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/siteassets/case-documents/uploads/opinions/final/coa/20160209_c324261(38)_rptr_19o-324261-final.pdf); [Legal Examiner](https://www.legalexaminer.com/smith-johnson/health/chiropractic-care-reimbursement-restricted-by-no-fault-act)
- **The fix.** HB 4449 (Rep. Beth Griffin), enacted as **Public Act 104 of 2020** and signed July 1, 2020, removed the 3107b(b) limitation. Expanded chiropractic coverage applies to services on or after **July 2, 2021**, and those services are subject to the fee schedule — [Plunkett Cooney](https://www.plunkettcooney.com/transportation-law-hub/chiropractic-coverage-michigan-No-Fault); [House Fiscal Agency analysis of HB 4449](https://hercules-milenia.legislature.mi.gov/documents/2019-2020/billanalysis/House/htm/2019-HLA-4449-F3CF73F6.htm); [Senate Fiscal Agency analysis](https://ombudsman.legislature.mi.gov/documents/2019-2020/billanalysis/Senate/pdf/2019-SFA-4449-F.pdf)
- No source found supports the idea that chiropractic coverage is tied to workers' comp. A search for a 3107b and workers'-disability-compensation link returned only the 2009-scope rule above.

**Minnesota: basic economic loss benefits (Minn. Stat. 65B.44)**
- $40,000 per person, split into **$20,000 medical** and $20,000 non-medical (wage loss, replacement services, etc.). Chiropractic is paid from the medical pool — [Nicolet Law](https://nicoletlaw.com/blog/minnesotas-no-fault-insurance-affects-your-car-accident-claim/); [The Complete Lawyer](https://thecompletelawyer.com/minnesota-car-accident-lawyer/)
- Non-medical benefits: wage loss at 85% of gross income, capped at $500/week; replacement services up to $200/week — [Nicolet Law](https://nicoletlaw.com/blog/minnesotas-no-fault-insurance-affects-your-car-accident-claim/); [McEllistrem Fargione no-fault overview (Jan 2022)](http://mcfarg.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/NO-FAULT-2022-updated-Jan-2022.pdf)
- 65B.44 requires reimbursement of "all reasonable expenses" for necessary chiropractic services — [Minn. Ct. App., Rodriguez v State Farm, A17-1800 (2018)](https://mn.gov/law-library-stat/archive/ctappub/2018/OPa171800-070218.pdf)
- **Workers' comp treatment parameters:**
  - Minn. R. 5221.6200 generally limits chiropractic adjustment to **12 weeks**, with more allowed if conditions are met — [Minn. R. 5221.6200](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/rules/5221.6200/); [Rodriguez COA opinion](https://mn.gov/law-library-stat/archive/ctappub/2018/OPa171800-070218.pdf)
  - In *Rodriguez v State Farm* (Minn. 2019), a workers' comp carrier stopped paying chiropractic after 12 weeks under those parameters. The no-fault claim was litigated separately — [FindLaw, Rodriguez (Minn. 2019)](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/mn-supreme-court/2012868.html)
  - Exact holding not verified this session (see Gaps): the reported question was whether a workers' comp parameter-based denial binds the no-fault insurer.

**North Dakota: basic no-fault benefits (N.D.C.C. 26.1-41)**
- **$30,000** maximum basic no-fault benefits for all economic loss to one person from one accident. Work loss is capped at **$150/week** and funeral expenses at **$3,500** — [N.D.C.C. ch. 26.1-41 (PDF)](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t26-1c41.pdf); [FindLaw 26.1-41-01](https://codes.findlaw.com/nd/title-26-1-insurance/nd-cent-code-sect-26-1-41-01/); [Nolo ND](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/north-dakota-no-fault-car-insurance.html)

### Inferences
- **Michigan data model.** A MI case needs these fields:
  - PIP tier (unlimited / 500k / 250k / 250k-with-exclusions / 50k-Medicaid / opted-out / assigned-claims 250k)
  - Policy issue/renewal date
  - Accident date (pre- vs post-June 11, 2019; see Andary in section 7)
  - Date of service (this picks the fee-schedule percentage)
  - Billing basis: Medicare amount × % vs chargemaster × % with the CPI factor
- **Configure fee percentages as dated rows** (effective_from, effective_to, provider_category, pct), not as a constant. Florida's 80% coinsurance has no MI equivalent. Michigan pays the lesser of the billed charge or the fee-schedule cap, with no 80%/20% split (inferred from the statute's structure).
- **Chiropractic in MI.** For dates of service on or after July 2, 2021, chiropractic should be treated like any other provider service under the fee schedule. For older dates of service, flag extremity, modality and "beyond the spine" services as possibly non-covered.
- **MN and ND are much simpler:**
  - MN: one $20k medical bucket to track against.
  - ND: one $30k bucket shared with wage loss and other economic loss. The clinic's bills compete with the patient's wage claim.
  - The app needs a running "remaining benefits" balance per claim.

### Gaps
- Full current text of MCL 500.3157 could not be fetched: subsections, provider-category definitions, and the exact 55%/54%/52.5% chargemaster step-down dates. Confirm at https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=mcl-500-3157
- CPI adjustment percentages for July 2024–2025 and July 2025–2026 dates of service were not found. The DIFS bulletins issued each year should hold them.
- Exact definitions of the "$250k with exclusions" and "opt-out" options in MCL 500.3107d (qualified health coverage, Medicare Parts A and B) were not verified this session. *(Background knowledge, unverified):* the opt-out is available to a named insured who has Medicare Parts A and B, with spouse and resident relatives covered by qualified health coverage. The $250k-with-exclusions option lets people with qualified health coverage exclude themselves from PIP medical.
- Whether MN or ND has a statutory no-fault fee schedule. *(Background knowledge, unverified):*
  - MN 65B.44 subd. 2 reportedly limits medical charges to those "customarily" charged for comparable non-injury cases.
  - ND 26.1-41-01's definition of "medical expenses" reportedly uses "reasonable charges."
  - Neither state is believed to have a Medicare-based schedule.
- Exact holding of *Rodriguez v State Farm* (Minn. 2019) on whether workers' comp treatment parameters bind no-fault insurers.

## 2. Deadlines: notice of claim, provider direct actions, insurer payment deadlines and interest

### Takeaway
- **Michigan**
  - Notice: the one-year notice rule, with an action allowed within one year of the most recent allowable expense.
  - One-year-back rule: tolled since June 11, 2019 from the specific claim until the insurer's formal denial. Not retroactive to pre-amendment claims.
  - Since 2019 PA 21, providers have a direct cause of action under MCL 500.3112.
  - Interest: 12% simple interest on benefits overdue 30 days after reasonable proof.
- **Minnesota:** benefits overdue 30 days after reasonable proof, then 15% simple interest (65B.54).
- **North Dakota:** payment and limitations rules could not be verified this session.

### Cited Findings

**Michigan: notice and the one-year-back rule (MCL 500.3145)**
- The 2019 amendment added tolling. The one-year-back period is "tolled from the date of a specific claim for payment of the benefits until the date the insurer formally denies the claim" — [Segal McCambridge](https://www.segalmccambridge.com/blog/michigan-no-fault-acts-new-but-old-one-year-back-rule-and-the-requirement-of-a-formal-denial-of-a-claim/); [Garan Lucow, No-Fault Reform Series: 3145](https://www.garanlucow.com/publication/no-fault-reform-series-3145/); [Plunkett Cooney](https://www.plunkettcooney.com/transportation-law-hub/court-clarifies-new-tolling-provision-of-michigan-no-fault)
- The tolling amendment does **not** apply retroactively to claims for services that accrued before its effective date, even when suit was filed afterward. Sources disagree on whether the Court of Appeals or the Supreme Court made the final ruling — [Speaker Law Firm](https://www.speakerlaw.com/blog/michigan-supreme-court-rules-tolling-amendment-does-not-apply-retroactively-to-pre-amendment-no-fault-claims); [Garan Lucow, Encompass v Citizens](https://www.garanlucow.com/publication/michigan-court-reaffirms-no-retroactive-tolling/); [COA opinion Nov 17, 2022](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/siteassets/case-documents/uploads/OPINIONS/FINAL/COA/20221117_C357225_46_357225.OPN.PDF)
- For claims accruing before June 11, 2019, *Devillers* (2005) applies: the one-year-back rule is enforced as written, with no judicial tolling — [amicus brief, MSC 165445](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/494846/siteassets/case-documents/briefs/msc/2023-2024/165445/165445_73_ac_brf-iam.pdf)

**Michigan: provider direct action (MCL 500.3112)**
- *Covenant v State Farm* (2017) held that providers had no statutory cause of action against no-fault insurers — [Bodman](https://www.bodmanlaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/2017.06.01_MI_S._Ct._Strips_HC_Providers_of_COA_Against_No_Fault_Insurers_Rheaume.pdf); [Federalist Society docket watch](https://fedsoc.org/commentary/fedsoc-blog/docket-watch-covenant-v-state-farm)
- **2019 PA 21** amended MCL 500.3112 to give providers an independent statutory cause of action against the insurer to recover overdue benefits — [COA opinion Mar 2, 2023](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/siteassets/case-documents/uploads/OPINIONS/FINAL/COA/20230302_C359371_36_359371.OPN.PDF); [michbar COA 2025 (Centria)](https://www.michbar.org/Portals/0/opinions/appeals/2025/022425/83248.pdf)
- 2025 COA (*Centria*): a provider suing under amended 3112 must show the insured actually **incurred** the expenses. The MSC reversed an earlier Centria COA ruling in 2024, so this line of cases is still moving — [michbar COA Feb 24, 2025](https://www.michbar.org/Portals/0/opinions/appeals/2025/022425/83248.pdf)

**Michigan: payment deadline and interest (MCL 500.3142)**
- 12% per annum simple interest when benefits are not paid within 30 days after reasonable proof of loss. This was surfaced only via a search summary of a law-firm article; the statute text was not fetched.
- The DIFS utilization review rules also award section 3142 interest on overdue amounts when a provider wins a UR appeal — [Mich Admin Code R 500.65 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/michigan/Mich-Admin-Code-R-500-65)

**Minnesota (Minn. Stat. 65B.54)**
- Benefits are overdue if not paid within **30 days** after the insurer receives reasonable proof of the fact and amount of loss. An insurer may instead accumulate claims for up to 31 days and pay within 15 days after the accumulation period — [Minn. Stat. 65B.54](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/65b.54)
- Overdue amounts bear **15% per annum simple interest** — [Minn. Stat. 65B.54](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/65b.54)
- If the insurer complies with the electronic transaction standards of Minn. Stat. 62J.536, the provider may not directly bill the insured for amounts not remitted under those standards — [Minn. Stat. 65B.54](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/65b.54)

**North Dakota**
- No primary source was found this session for the ND payment deadline, interest rate, notice requirement or limitations period. The 26.1-41 index lists 26.1-41-12 as "Discovery of facts about an injured person," not an overdue-benefits section — [ndlegis.gov Century Code index](https://ndlegis.gov/general-information/north-dakota-century-code/index.html)
- The ND judgment interest rate for 2026 is **10.00%** (N.D.C.C. 28-20-34). This is **not** a no-fault overdue-benefit rate — [ND Courts, interest rate on judgments](https://www.ndcourts.gov/state-court-administration/interest-rate-on-judgments)

### Inferences
- **Michigan claim clocks:** the app should run these per claim:
  - Date the insurer received "reasonable proof" (bill with records), then the 30-day overdue date, then 12% interest accrual.
  - The one-year-back window from each date of service.
  - A tolling flag from claim submission until the insurer's formal denial, for dates of service after June 11, 2019.
  - The one-year notice deadline from the accident.
- **Minnesota:** the same 30-day "reasonable proof" clock, with 15% simple interest. If the clinic bills electronically under 62J.536 and the insurer complies, unpaid amounts may not be billed to the patient. This matters for balance billing (see section 6).

### Gaps
- **Michigan 3145 core text.** The search did not return the current text: written notice within one year of the accident, and suit within one year of the most recent allowable expense. *(Background knowledge, unverified):*
  - Written notice to the insurer within 1 year after the accident, unless benefits were already paid.
  - Recovery limited to losses incurred within 1 year before suit, subject to the tolling above.
- **MCL 500.3142 text.** Not fetched. *(Background knowledge, unverified):*
  - Provider bills must be submitted on the required form/codes.
  - Separately, the 2019 reform made benefits overdue if a bill is not paid within 30 days, or if claims of a minor or incompetent person are not paid.
  - Bills submitted more than 1 year after service may be denied.
- **MN 65B.55 notice requirement and the 65B.56 IME and records rules.** Not returned. *(Background knowledge, unverified):*
  - Notice to the insurer "as soon as practicable," with a 6-month limit after the accident under 65B.55.
  - 65B.56 permits IMEs and discovery of medical records.
- **MN limitations period for no-fault benefit claims.** Not verified. *(Background knowledge, unverified):* commonly cited as 6 years.
- **ND.** No verified source for any of these:
  - when benefits become overdue (*background, unverified:* 30 days, under 26.1-41-12 or a nearby section)
  - the interest rate
  - the statute of limitations for basic no-fault benefits (*background, unverified:* 4 years from the accident, or 4 years from the last payment)
  - Check https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t26-1c41.pdf

## 3. Required forms and claim processes

### Takeaway
- **Michigan:** UR appeals use DIFS form **FIS 2356**. Billing must follow the DIFS billing guidance (Bulletin 2025-11-INS, superseding 2024-06-INS). No official copy of the insurer "Application for Benefits" was found.
- **MN and ND:** no specific state-mandated claim forms were found this session.

### Cited Findings
- **FIS 2356:** the DIFS provider utilization-review appeal form. It is filed with DIFS (DIFS-URAppeals@michigan.gov) within 90 days of the insurer's determination or bill denial — [FIS 2356 (06/26)](https://www.michigan.gov/-/media/Project/Websites/difs/Form/Insurance/UR/FIS_2356.pdf?rev=af1e18a49493443da13be4e20fda75e2)
- **DIFS UR FAQ and guide:** [DIFS UR FAQ](https://www.michigan.gov/difs/utilization-review/faq); [UR FAQ and Guide PDF](https://www.michigan.gov/difs/-/media/Project/Websites/difs/ORRA/UR_FAQ_and_Guide.pdf)
- **Bulletin 2025-11-INS:** the current DIFS payment and billing guidance for no-fault insurers and providers. It supersedes 2024-06-INS — [autonofaultlaw.com bulletin summaries](https://autonofaultlaw.com/insurance-bulletin-summaries/)
- **MN e-billing:** Minnesota ties provider billing to the 62J.536 electronic transaction standards — [Minn. Stat. 65B.54](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/65b.54)

### Inferences
- The MI app should store, per claim:
  - the insurer's Application for Benefits status (received / submitted)
  - each insurer determination date (this starts the 90-day FIS 2356 appeal clock)
  - the bulletin version used for billing rules
- For MN, store the e-billing (837) submission date as the "reasonable proof" date.

### Gaps
- The standard Michigan "Application for Benefits" is insurer-specific; there is no single state form. The Michigan Assigned Claims Plan (MACP/MAIPF) has its own application. Neither could be fetched. *(Background knowledge, unverified)*
- The DIFS billing format rule (CMS-1500 / UB-04 required by the 2019 amendments to 3157) was not verified.
- **MN:** whether insurers use a standard "Application for No-Fault Benefits" form, and medical-authorization rules.
- **ND:** claim form and process, and any ND Insurance Department complaint process. Not found.

## 4. Utilization review, IMEs and arbitration

### Takeaway
- **Michigan:** DIFS UR rules (Mich Admin Code R 500.61–500.69).
  - An insurer can request an explanation; the provider must respond within 30 days.
  - The insurer issues written notice of an overutilization or inappropriate-cost determination within 30 days of the provider's explanation.
  - The provider may appeal to DIFS within 90 days. DIFS decides on the written record within 28 days of the reply, extendable by 28 days. Judicial review is under MCL 500.244.
- **Minnesota:** binding arbitration is mandatory for no-fault claims of **$10,000 or less** (65B.525).
- **North Dakota:** nothing found.

### Cited Findings

**Michigan UR**
- **Provider explanation:** when the insurer asks for a written explanation, the provider must respond within **30 days** of receipt — [DIFS UR rules PDF](https://www.michigan.gov/difs/-/media/Project/Websites/difs/ORRA/Utilization_Review_Rules.pdf); [CEF Lawyers summary](https://ceflawyers.com/blog/difs-has-issued-rules-for-utilization-reviews-heres-what-you-need-to-know/)
- **Insurer determination notice:** if the insurer finds the treatment overutilized or inappropriate, or the cost inappropriate, it must give written notice within **30 days** after receiving the provider's explanation. The notice must include the criteria used, the amount paid, and any extra records needed for reconsideration — [DIFS UR rules](https://www.michigan.gov/difs/-/media/Project/Websites/difs/ORRA/Utilization_Review_Rules.pdf)
- **DIFS appeal** — [R 500.65 (Cornell)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/michigan/Mich-Admin-Code-R-500-65); [FIS 2356](https://www.michigan.gov/-/media/Project/Websites/difs/Form/Insurance/UR/FIS_2356.pdf?rev=af1e18a49493443da13be4e20fda75e2)
  - Filed within **90 days** of the determination, on the DIFS form.
  - DIFS notifies the insurer and the injured person within **14 days**.
  - The insurer may reply within **21 days**.
  - The director decides within **28 days** after the reply (or after the reply deadline), extendable by **28 days**.
- **Decision basis and relief:** the decision is based on written submissions. A provider who wins gets 3142 interest. Judicial review is under MCL 500.244(1) — [R 500.65](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/michigan/Mich-Admin-Code-R-500-65)
- **Rule numbering:** numbering differs between the 2020 JCAR draft and the final rules, so verify subsection cites — [JCAR package 2019-136](https://council.legislature.mi.gov/JCAR/File?path=%2FJCARFiles%2F2020+Documents+Received%2FFinal+JCAR+Packages%2F2019-136+IF+FinalRule_StrikeBold_Version.doc.htm)

**Minnesota arbitration (65B.525)**
- Binding arbitration is mandatory for all cases where the claim at the start of arbitration is **$10,000 or less** against a reparation obligor for no-fault benefits (or comprehensive/collision) — [Minn. Stat. 65B.525 (FindLaw)](https://codes.findlaw.com/mn/insurance-ch-59a-79a/mn-st-sect-65b-525/); [Revisor 2014 PDF](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/2014/cite/65B.525/pdf)
- Conciliation (small claims) court therefore cannot hear these claims — [The Complete Lawyer](https://thecompletelawyer.com/minnesota-car-accident-lawyer/)

### Inferences
- **Michigan workflow states:** UR request received → explanation due (+30d) → insurer determination (+30d) → DIFS appeal window (+90d) → DIFS decision (+14 +21 +28, optional +28) → judicial review. Each step needs a deadline field.
- **Minnesota:** route any denied claim of $10,000 or less to an "arbitration (AAA / Minnesota no-fault)" track, not court. Store the claim amount at the start of arbitration.

### Gaps
- **MN IME rules (65B.56):** not retrieved. *(Background knowledge, unverified):* the insured must submit to examinations by physicians selected by the insurer, and insurers frequently cut off chiropractic after an IME.
- **MN arbitration administration:** AAA administers no-fault arbitration under the Minnesota No-Fault Arbitration Rules. Not verified. *(Background knowledge)*
- **ND:** UR, IME and arbitration rules not found.

## 5. Chiropractic-specific limits and visit caps

### Takeaway
- **Michigan:** no statutory visit cap was found. Since July 2, 2021 (PA 104 of 2020), the old 2009-scope exclusion is gone. Chiropractic care is limited only by reasonable necessity, the fee schedule and utilization review.
- **Minnesota:** no statutory no-fault visit cap. The workers' comp treatment parameters (12 weeks of adjustments, Minn. R. 5221.6200) come up in disputes but govern workers' comp.
- **North Dakota:** no chiropractic-specific provision was found.

### Cited Findings
- **MI:** pre-July 2, 2021 dates of service — only spinal chiropractic within the 2009 scope was covered (*Measel*; *Hoffman v Auto Club* 1995 limited care to the spine) — [Legal Examiner](https://www.legalexaminer.com/smith-johnson/health/chiropractic-care-reimbursement-restricted-by-no-fault-act); [Plunkett Cooney](https://www.plunkettcooney.com/transportation-law-hub/chiropractic-coverage-michigan-No-Fault)
- **MI:** PA 104 of 2020 removed that limit for services on or after July 2, 2021 — [Plunkett Cooney](https://www.plunkettcooney.com/transportation-law-hub/chiropractic-coverage-michigan-No-Fault)
- **MN:** the 12-week chiropractic adjustment parameter is in Minn. R. 5221.6200 (workers' comp), with exceptions — [Minn. R. 5221.6200](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/rules/5221.6200/)
- **MN:** an unpublished COA decision (*Gjevre*) held the parameters did not govern where the workers' comp carrier had denied liability. It is non-binding — [minnlawyer full text](https://minnlawyer.com/wp-files/fulltext-031212/opa111344-030512.html)

### Inferences
- Do not hard-code visit caps for any of the three states. Do add MI UR flags and MN "IME cutoff" tracking.

### Gaps
- Whether any MN or ND insurer-side regulation imposes visit limits. None was found.

## 6. Tort thresholds and case-value tracking

### Takeaway
- **Michigan:** "serious impairment of body function," permanent serious disfigurement, or death (MCL 500.3135). *McCormick v Carrier* (2010) sets a fairly lenient test. The 2019 reform also lets the injured person sue for medical expenses above their PIP limit.
- **Minnesota:** medical expenses over **$4,000**, excluding diagnostic X-rays and rehabilitation; or permanent injury, permanent disfigurement, death, or **60+ days** of disability (65B.51).
- **North Dakota:** medical expenses over **$2,500**; or death, dismemberment, serious and permanent disfigurement, or disability beyond **60 days** (26.1-41-01 "serious injury").

### Cited Findings
- **MN monetary threshold:** reasonable medical expense over **$4,000**, excluding diagnostic X-rays and rehabilitative services — [Nicolet Law](https://nicoletlaw.com/blog/minnesotas-no-fault-insurance-affects-your-car-accident-claim/); [The Complete Lawyer](https://thecompletelawyer.com/minnesota-car-accident-lawyer/)
- **MN verbal threshold:** permanent injury, permanent disfigurement, death, or disability of 60+ days — [Nicolet Law](https://nicoletlaw.com/blog/minnesotas-no-fault-insurance-affects-your-car-accident-claim/)
- **ND:** "Serious injury" means "an accidental bodily injury which results in death, dismemberment, serious and permanent disfigurement or disability beyond sixty days, or medical expenses in excess of two thousand five hundred dollars" — [N.D.C.C. 26.1-41-01 (FindLaw)](https://codes.findlaw.com/nd/title-26-1-insurance/nd-cent-code-sect-26-1-41-01/); [N.D.C.C. ch. 26.1-41](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t26-1c41.pdf)
- **ND history:** a 2003 Legislative Council draft proposed raising the threshold to $4,000 and excluding diagnostic testing. It is a draft, not current law — [ND Legis 2003 draft](https://ndlegis.gov/assembly/58-2003/interim/FAEO0200.pdf)
- **MI excess economic damages:** once PIP coverage is exhausted, the injured person may sue the at-fault driver for excess economic damages — [Michigan Auto Law](https://www.michiganautolaw.com/no-fault/no-fault-act/pip-medical-benefits-coverage/)
- **MI fee schedule in third-party claims:** *Canty v Mason* (COA) held the fee schedule applies to third-party claims for excess medical expenses — [michiganlegalcenter.com](https://www.michiganlegalcenter.com/mcl-§-500-3157-medical-fee-schedules-for-pip-charges-post-2019-reform-2); see also [COA Apr 18, 2024](https://www.michbar.org/Portals/0/opinions/appeals/2024/041824/81444.pdf)

### Inferences
- **MN and ND thresholds are partly dollar-based.** The app should keep a running "threshold-countable medical" total per case:
  - MN: exclude diagnostic X-rays and rehab from the count.
  - ND: count all medical expenses.
  - Alert at $4,000 (MN) and $2,500 (ND).
  - Also track disability-days, with alerts at 60 consecutive days for MN and over 60 days for ND.
  - This replaces Florida's "permanent injury" flag as the main case-value trigger.
- **Michigan threshold is qualitative.** Track objective findings and documented impairment (the *McCormick* elements below), plus a flag for medical bills above the policy limit. Under *Canty*, those excess amounts are themselves capped at fee-schedule value.

### Gaps
- **MCL 500.3135 / *McCormick* text** did not come back in search. *(Background knowledge, unverified):* *McCormick v Carrier*, 487 Mich 180 (2010), requires:
  - an objectively manifested impairment,
  - of an important body function,
  - that affects the person's general ability to lead his or her normal life.
  The 2019 reform (PA 21) wrote this test into 3135(5). No minimum duration is required.

## 7. Balance billing, liens and letters of protection

### Takeaway
- **Michigan:** the fee schedule caps what a provider can be paid "for" treatment. No authority quoted here was confirmed to bar a provider from billing the patient for the balance (see Gaps).
- **Minnesota:** a provider may not directly bill the insured for amounts the insurer did not remit when the insurer complies with the 62J.536 e-transaction standards.
- **North Dakota:** nothing found.
- No verified provider-lien statute was found for any of the three states.

### Cited Findings
- **MI:** MCL 500.3157 limits what a provider may be paid for PIP-covered treatment to the fee-schedule amount. The same limit applies in third-party excess-expense claims (*Canty v Mason*) — [michiganlegalcenter.com](https://www.michiganlegalcenter.com/mcl-§-500-3157-medical-fee-schedules-for-pip-charges-post-2019-reform-2)
- **MI:** courts have addressed fee-schedule questions alongside a duty to mitigate — [Plunkett Cooney, fee schedule duty to mitigate](https://plunkettcooney.com/transportation-law-hub/fee-schedule-duty-to-mitigate)
- **MN:** when the reparation obligor complies with Minn. Stat. 62J.536, the provider may not directly bill the insured for amounts not remitted — [Minn. Stat. 65B.54](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/65b.54)

### Inferences
- The app should not generate patient balance bills for MI no-fault claims above fee-schedule amounts.
- Treat Florida-style "letters of protection" as attorney-side contracts only. In MI and MN these are most relevant when PIP is exhausted or denied, or for opt-out patients whose health insurer is primary.
- The app must track which payer is primary: PIP, health insurance (MI opt-out or $250k with exclusions), or Medicare/Medicaid (MI $50k Medicaid option).

### Gaps
- **MI 3157 balance-billing text.** Exact wording not fetched. *(Background knowledge, unverified):* 3157(2) says a provider "is not eligible for payment or reimbursement under this chapter of more than" the schedule. MCL 500.3157(4) or a related subsection is reported to bar billing the injured person for the difference when the insurer paid at the schedule.
- **MI hospital/health lien statutes:** applicability to chiropractors not researched.
- **MN:** whether MN has a provider lien statute that covers chiropractors. Not found.
- **ND:** balance-billing and lien rules. Not found.

## 8. Recent reforms (2019–2026), court decisions and pending changes

### Takeaway
- *Andary v USAA* (MSC, July 2023) held the 2019 fee schedule and attendant-care limits do **not** apply to people injured before June 11, 2019. Their rights vested at the time of injury.
- *Demske v Fick* (MSC No. 167153) asks whether the fee schedule applies to post-reform claims. It was argued, but no decision was found.
- SB 530/531 (2023) would roll back the attendant-care and fee-schedule cuts. The Senate passed them on Oct 19, 2023; no enactment was found.
- Current DIFS billing guidance is Bulletin 2025-11-INS.
- Minnesota and North Dakota: no recent reforms were found.

### Cited Findings
- ***Andary v USAA*:** the policies and statutes in effect when Andary and Krueger were injured control their PIP entitlement, not 2019 PA 21/22. Rights vest at the latest on the date of injury. The 2019 fee schedule and attendant-care limits do not apply retroactively — [Andary opinion (MSC 2023)](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4a2661/siteassets/case-documents/opinions-orders/msc-term-opinions-(manually-curated)/22-23/andary-op.pdf); [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/michigan/supreme-court/2023/164772.html); [Michigan Auto Law, Aug 8, 2023](https://www.michiganautolaw.com/blog/2023/08/08/andary-v-usaa-michigan-supreme-court-ruling-no-fault-changes-do-not-apply-retroactively/); [Claims Journal, Aug 1, 2023](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/midwest/2023/08/01/318370.htm)
- **Later rulings in line with Andary:** the MSC reaffirmed that the fee schedule and attendant-care limits do not apply to pre-reform losses — [CEF Lawyers, "More of the Same"](https://ceflawyers.com/blog/more-of-the-same-michigan-supreme-court-affirms-the-no-fault-fee-schedule-and-attendant-care-limitations-do-not-apply-pre-reform-losses/)
- ***Demske v Fick* (MSC 167153):**
  - The question is whether the 3157 fee schedules apply where the policy renewal, accident and treatment all came after June 2019.
  - The State argues the schedules apply by statute and by contract.
  - No decision was found as of Oct 2026 searches.
  - Sources: [State supplemental brief](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4ad870/siteassets/case-documents/briefs/msc/2024-2025/167153/167153_58_01_ae_supp_brf.pdf); [appellant brief](https://www.courts.michigan.gov/4969bc/siteassets/case-documents/briefs/msc/2024-2025/167153/167153_66_01_ac_brf-maj.pdf); [citizenportal.ai summary of argument](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/6562751/Michigan/Judicial/Supreme-Court-Judicial-Rulings-Opinions/Supreme-Court-hears-dispute-over-whether-no-fault-fee-schedules-apply-to-Demski-injuries)
- **SB 530/531 (2023):** introduced September 2023 and passed by the Senate Oct 19, 2023. No House action or enactment was found — [Michigan HomeCare & Hospice Assn](https://www.mhha.org/auto-no-fault/about-auto-no-fault/); [SFA analysis SB 530](https://ombudsman.legislature.mi.gov/documents/2023-2024/billanalysis/Senate/pdf/2023-SFA-0530-G.pdf)
- **PA 104 of 2020:** the chiropractic scope fix, effective for services on or after July 2, 2021 — [Plunkett Cooney](https://www.plunkettcooney.com/transportation-law-hub/chiropractic-coverage-michigan-No-Fault)
- **DIFS Section 271 report (2025)** on reform effects — [DIFS Section 271 Report 2025](https://www.michigan.gov/autoinsurance/-/media/Project/Websites/autoinsurance/PDFs/AutoInsurance/Section_271_Report_2025.pdf)
- **ND:** a 1997 bill (HB 1273) proposed 30 days' notice before terminating basic no-fault benefits. Enactment is unknown — [ND Legis 1997](https://ndlegis.gov/assembly/55-1997/regular/bill-text/HARJ0300.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Flag every MI case with three dates:** accident date, policy issue/renewal date and date of service.
  - Pre-June 11, 2019 injuries: no fee schedule (*Andary*), with "reasonable charge" billing.
  - Post-reform cases: fee schedule applies, subject to the outcome of *Demske*.
- Watch for a *Demske* decision and any SB 530-type legislation. Either could change fee-schedule application.
