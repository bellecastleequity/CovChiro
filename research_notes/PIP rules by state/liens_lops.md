## 1. Which states have statutory provider liens that cover chiropractors? (who, perfection steps, deadlines, caps, patient liability)

### Takeaway
States fall into three groups. (a) Some statutes expressly name chiropractors or "healing arts" providers: Georgia since 2023, Montana, Oklahoma, Louisiana, Arizona (all non-hospital providers) and Colorado (health-care provider lien act). (b) Some cover only "physicians" and leave chiropractors ambiguous or excluded: Illinois (chiropractors arguably "licensed physicians"), Washington, North Carolina, Nebraska (court says excluded), Virginia, Kansas and Massachusetts. (c) Some cover hospitals or EMS only: Texas, Nevada and Oregon (Oregon also covers physicians, PAs and NPs, but not chiropractors). Perfection almost always requires written notice before money is paid out, often by certified mail and often with a county filing. Missing a step usually voids the lien against the settlement but not the patient's underlying debt.

**Detailed findings by state**

- **Georgia (strongest chiropractor-specific statute):** SB 168 (2023, effective July 1, 2023) added chiropractic practices to the hospital lien statute O.C.G.A. 44-14-470 et seq. Chiropractic liens are subject to hospital liens, and providers must first submit bills to the patient's health insurer — [OBF Legal summary](https://obflegal.com/2023/09/georgia-hospital-lien-law-adds-chiropractors-billing-changes/); [SB 168 signed text](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2023-signed-legislation/sb-168/download); [44-14-470 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-14/article-8/part-8/section-44-14-470/)
- Georgia perfection: written notice to the patient and, to the best of the claimant's knowledge, to liable parties and insurers at least 15 days before filing, stating the lien is not a lien against the patient's property. Then file a verified statement with the superior court clerk in the provider's county and the patient's county of residence. The statement includes the patient's name and address from provider records and the provider's name and location. Physician and chiropractic practices must file within 90 days after the person first sought treatment for the injury; hospitals, nursing homes and burn centers within 75 days after discharge — [O.C.G.A. 44-14-471 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-14/article-8/part-8/section-44-14-471/); [Studstill Firm](https://www.studstillfirm.com/blog/2025/february/medical-liens/)
- Georgia consequence: failure to timely perfect invalidates the lien, except against a liable party that received actual notice before release or settlement — [44-14-471 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-14/article-8/part-8/section-44-14-471/). Enforcement action and limitation are in [44-14-473](https://syfert.com/georgia/code/44-14-473.html) (period not captured; verify).
- **Arizona:** A.R.S. 33-931 gives health care providers a lien for reasonable and customary charges against the injured person's tort recovery — [azleg.gov 33-931](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/33/00931.htm); [JSH treatise ch. 12 (2024)](https://www.jshfirm.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/v28Final-Chapter-12-Health-Care-Provider-Liens-1.pdf)
- Arizona perfection (33-932): non-hospital providers must record the lien before, or within 30 days after, the patient first receives services (*Premier Physicians Group v. Navarro*, Ariz. 2016). Within 5 days after recording, mail a copy by first-class mail to the injured person and to all persons and insurers claimed to be liable. An unrecorded lien is invalid and cannot be enforced under 33-934 — [33-932 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/arizona/title-33/section-33-932/); [Quarles on Navarro](https://www.quarles.com/newsroom/publications/arizona-supreme-court-clarifies-deadline-by-which-non-hospital-health-care-providers-must-record-liens)
- Arizona release: provider must release the lien within 30 days of payment (33-936) — [Law for Seniors](https://lawforseniors.org/topics/money-and-debt/debt-collection/health-care-provider-liens)
- Arizona in-network rule: if the provider contracts with the patient's health insurer, the contract must expressly allow the lien; otherwise the lien is invalid — [azleg.gov 33-931](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/33/00931.htm)
- **Colorado:** The Health-Care Provider Lien Act, C.R.S. 38-27.5-101 et seq., covers chiropractors. Liens are limited to usual and customary billed charges, and the attorney's charging lien has priority — [Black & Blink 2026 update (search summary)](https://www.blackandblinklaw.com/blog/2026/05/how-colorado-chiropractors-can-properly-perfect-and-protect-their-medical-liens-in-personal-injury-cases-2026-update/)
- Colorado 38-27.5-103: an assignee takes the provider's rights and obligations; assignment terms are inadmissible; recovery for lien-based treatment is capped at usual and customary billed charges. Applies to third-party and UM/UIM claims — [38-27.5-103 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-38/liens/article-27-5/section-38-27-5-103/)
- Colorado HB21-1300: a provider or assignee creating a lien must advise the injured person of payment options, including use of insurance benefits — [leg.colorado.gov HB21-1300](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb21-1300). The Colorado Chiropractic Association publishes lien-form cautions — [CCA lien form materials (2024)](https://coloradochiropractic.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Lien-Form-materials.pdf)
- **Louisiana:** R.S. 9:4752 gives a health care provider, hospital or ambulance service a privilege for reasonable charges on the net recovery (judgment, settlement, compromise) and net insurance payable. The attorney's privilege has precedence — [legis.la.gov RS 9:4752](https://legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=108040). A secondary summary says the covered-provider definition includes licensed chiropractors — [LawServer 9:4752](https://www.lawserver.com/law/state/louisiana/la-laws/louisiana_revised_statutes_9-4752)
- Louisiana notice (9:4753): the privilege takes effect only on written notice before payment. The notice contains the patient's name and address and the provider's name and location. Send by certified mail RRR, or by fax with proof of receipt, to the injured person, their attorney, the liable party and insurers — [legis.la.gov RS 9:4753](https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=108041)
- **Montana:** MCA 71-3-1111 to -1118 (the "Lien Act") expressly lists chiropractors among claimants. The lien arises upon notice and attaches to all insurer proceeds except property damage — [71-3-1114](https://leg.mt.gov/bills/mca/title_0710/chapter_0030/part_0110/section_0140/0710-0030-0110-0140.html)
- Montana notice (71-3-1115): written notice to the person and any insurer stating the nature of services, recipient, dates, value, and that a lien is claimed — [71-3-1115](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0710/chapter_0030/part_0110/section_0150/0710-0030-0110-0150.html). An insurer paying after notice without satisfying the lien is liable for the reasonable value — [71-3-1117](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0710/chapter_0030/part_0110/section_0170/0710-0030-0110-0170.html)
- **Oklahoma:** 42 O.S. §46(A) gives a lien to physicians and other licensed healing-arts providers, including chiropractors. It does not apply to workers' comp (Title 85A) and is inferior to the attorney's lien. §46(B) adds a lien on monies an insurer pays the patient — [42-46 (Justia 2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-42/section-42-46/)
- Oklahoma notice: written notice stating the amount claimed, insurance policies, and names and addresses of provider, patient and adverse party. File with the county clerk and send by registered or certified mail to the adverse party and patient before any payment. Enforce by suit in the district court of the filing county within 1 year after learning of the judgment or settlement — [42-46 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-42/section-42-46/); [OK Title 42 PDF](https://oksenate.gov/sites/default/files/2022-05/os42.pdf)
- Oklahoma pending change: HB 3898 (2021–22) would set the lien amount at "usual and customary"; enactment not confirmed — [HB3898 ENGR](https://www.oklegislature.gov/cf_pdf/2021-22%20ENGR/hB/HB3898%20ENGR.PDF). No 50% cap was found in §46.
- **Illinois:** The Health Care Services Lien Act (770 ILCS 23) caps all liens at 40% of the verdict, judgment, award, settlement or compromise. When the 40% threshold is met, health care professionals collectively are capped at 20% and providers at 20%, with unused share reallocated. A one-third limitation also applies. Notice must be served on both the injured person and the party against whom the claim exists — [770 ILCS 23 (Justia 2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/illinois/chapter-770/act-770-ilcs-23/); [ilga.gov](http://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=2498&ChapterID=63); [Kryder Law](https://www.kryderlaw.com/faqs/illinois-medical-lien-act-explained/)
- Illinois definition of "health care professional" in search results: licensed physician, dentist, optometrist, naprapath, clinical psychologist or physical therapist. Chiropractors are not separately named — [FindLaw 770 ILCS 23/10](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-770-liens/il-st-sect-770-23-10/). Illinois firm blogs nonetheless illustrate "chiropractor liens" under the Act — [Kryder Law](https://www.kryderlaw.com/faqs/illinois-medical-lien-act-explained/)
- **Kansas:** K.S.A. 65-406 is a hospital lien. Sources dispute whether non-hospital providers can use it; a federal filing contested its application to a therapy LLC — [D. Kan. filing](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ksd-2_09-cv-02140/pdf/USCOURTS-ksd-2_09-cv-02140-1.pdf); [Kevin McManus Law](https://www.kevinmcmanuslaw.com/faqs/what-are-hospital-liens-how-it-affects-your-case/)
- Kansas notice (65-407): written notice of the amount, patient name, accident date and hospital name and location is filed with the district court clerk in the hospital's county before payment, plus a copy to the patient by registered or certified mail. Strict compliance is required (*Via Christi v. Reed*, 298 Kan. 503 (2013)) — [ksrevisor 65-407](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch65/065_004_0007.html); [SB 424 (2014) amendment](https://kslegislature.gov/li_2014/b2013_14/measures/documents/sb424_enrolled.pdf)
- **Texas:** Tex. Prop. Code ch. 55 gives hospitals a lien only if the patient is admitted within 72 hours of the accident. It can include a physician's emergency hospital-care charges during the first 7 days. EMS liens are subject to a county population limit (575,000 in 2005 text vs 800,000 per a firm; verify) — [ch. 55 (Justia 2013)](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/2013/property-code/title-5/subtitle-b/chapter-55); [GoSuits](https://gosuits.com/knowledge-base/texas-hospital-liens-injury-claims/). Practitioner sources say chiropractors are not lien-eligible under ch. 55 — [1-800-Lion-Law](https://1800lionlaw.com/tx/hospital-lien-statute/)
- **Washington:** RCW 60.44 lien covers ambulance operators, hospitals, and "every duly licensed nurse, practitioner, physician, and surgeon" for traumatic injury. It attaches to claims against tortfeasors and insurers and excludes workers' comp. Total liens are capped at 25% of the recovery. File with the county auditor within 20 days (late filing reportedly OK if before settlement). Providers using liens must disclose this in billing practices — [RCW 60.44](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=60.44&full=true&pdf=true); [GoldDog Law](https://golddoglaw.com/medical-liens-washington/); [Adler Giersch provider blog](https://www.adlergiersch.com/provider-blog/should-health-care-providers-make-medical-liens-part-of-their-billing-practices/). Chiropractors are not expressly named, and coverage is disputed between the statute text and one blog.
- **Oregon:** ORS 87.555 covers hospitals, physicians, physician associates and nurse practitioners, not chiropractors (chiropractors are regulated under ORS ch. 684, not 677). File a notice in ORS 87.570 form with the county recording officer, reportedly within 30 days after discharge. If perfected, the insurer pays the hospital/physician directly and prorates if funds are insufficient — [oregonlegislature.gov ORS ch. 87](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors087.html); [ORS 87.555](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_87.555); [Johnson Law](https://johnsonlaw.com/medical-liens-101-hospitals-chiropractors-and-what-they-can-claim-from-your-settlement)
- **Massachusetts:** G.L. c.111 §70A is a hospital/HMO lien subordinate to the attorney's lien. §70B notice goes by certified mail RRR to the injured person, attorney, liable party and liability insurer before judgment or settlement. Sources conflict on deadlines (90 days after discharge per MWL chart vs 10 days of admission per MedicalAI) — [FindLaw c.111 §70A](https://codes.findlaw.com/ma/part-i-administration-of-the-government-ch-1-182/ma-gen-laws-ch-111-sect-70a/); [MWL 50-state chart (1/13/22)](https://www.mwl-law.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/HOSPITAL-LIEN-LAWS-IN-ALL-50-STATES-CHART-00215685x9EBBF.pdf); [Suffolk faculty article](https://dc.suffolk.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1205&context=suls-faculty). Attorneys or insurers who pay over lien funds can be liable to the provider — [Suffolk](https://dc.suffolk.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1205&context=suls-faculty)
- **Virginia:** Va. Code 8.01-66.2 caps liens at $2,500 for a hospital or nursing home, $750 per physician, nurse, PT or pharmacy, and $200 per EMS provider. The lien is not perfected without written notice to the attorney or at-fault party — [8.01-66.2 (FindLaw)](https://codes.findlaw.com/va/title-8-01-civil-remedies-and-procedure/va-code-sect-8-01-66-2/); [C. Pollard Law](https://cpollardlaw.com/personal-injury-attorney-virginia/health-care-provider-health-insurance-medical-liens/). Article 7.1 states "physician" includes chiropractor — [Va. Code Art. 7.1](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title8.01/chapter3/article7.1/). Under 8.01-27.5, a provider that does not submit bills to health insurance loses the 8.01-66.2 lien — [C. Pollard Law](https://cpollardlaw.com/personal-injury-attorney-virginia/health-care-provider-health-insurance-medical-liens/)
- **North Carolina:** G.S. 44-49/44-50 liens are capped at 50% of the recovery after attorney fees, shared pro rata (pro-rata requirement added 2003 after *Crowson*) — [G.S. 44-50](http://www.ncleg.net/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_44/GS_44-50.html); [NC State Bar 2017 FEO 4](https://www.ncbar.gov/for-lawyers/ethics-and-governing-rules/ethics-opinions/opinions/2017-formal-ethics-opinion-4/); [Collins Law](https://www.attydc.com/blog/statutory-lien/). NC courts have never directly addressed whether chiropractors can claim a 44-49 lien — [Wallace Pierce](https://www.wallacepierce.com/durham-car-accident-lawyer/compensation-for-injuries/liens-and-subrogation/physicians-liens-under-44-49/). The cap limits payment from the settlement, not the underlying debt — [Jeffrey Howard Law](https://www.jeffreyhowardlaw.com/your-settlement-v-your-bills/)
- **Nebraska:** 52-401 physician lien; a court annotation holds chiropractors are excluded by omission — [Nebraska Legislature 52-401](https://www.nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=52-401)
- **Nevada:** NRS 108.590–.610 is a hospital lien. Record before payment, and serve a certified copy by registered or certified mail on the responsible party and insurer before judgment or settlement — [NRS ch. 108](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/nrs-108.html); [NRS 108.610](https://www.legalfix.com/statutes/state-codes/nevada/chapter-108/statute-108-610). No chiropractor lien provision was found.
- **California:** Statutory hospital and county liens do not govern private providers like chiropractors, who operate on lien contracts — [search summary of practitioner sources](https://www.murphyprachthauser.com/blog/what-is-a-lien-should-i-sign-a-chiropractor-lien-or-a-doctor-lien/)
- **Attorney-lien priority:** attorney fee liens generally outrank hospital liens in AL, AK, AZ, AR, GA, ID, IN, IA, KS, LA, ME, MD, MA, MN, MO, MT, NE, NV, NM, NY, NC, OK, OR, RI, SD, TN, TX, UT, VT, VA, WI, DC — [MWL Hospital Lien Laws in All 50 States](https://www.mwl-law.com/resources/hospital-lien-laws-in-all-50-states/)
- **Patient remains liable:** In Massachusetts, failure to give notice may make the lien unenforceable against the settlement "though the underlying debt may remain" — [Bailey & Burke](https://www.baileyandburke.com/blog/how-to-remove-a-umass-memorial-lien-from-your-injury-settlement/). NC: capped lien does not extinguish the balance — [Jeffrey Howard Law](https://www.jeffreyhowardlaw.com/your-settlement-v-your-bills/). Georgia notice must state the lien is not against the patient's property — [44-14-471](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-14/article-8/part-8/section-44-14-471/)

### Inferences
- Illinois: chiropractors in Illinois are licensed under the Medical Practice Act as physicians ("chiropractic physicians"), so they very likely qualify as "licensed physician" under the HCSLA. This is my inference, not verified in this research; confirm with counsel.
- For the app, statutory-lien states need different perfection workflows: (i) notice-only (LA, MT, MA, VA, IL); (ii) notice plus county filing (GA, OK, KS, AZ recording, WA auditor, OR recorder, NV); (iii) contract-only (CA, FL, TX for chiros, OR for chiros, NE). Short "first-treatment" clocks (AZ 30 days, GA 90 days, WA 20 days) argue for an intake-triggered alert.
- Statutory caps create a mathematical ceiling that the app can compute: IL 40%/20%, NC 50% of net after fees, WA 25%, VA flat $750.

## 2. States without a usable chiropractor lien (LOP / contract lien states) and LOP restrictions and disclosure rules

### Takeaway
Where no statute covers chiropractors (notably FL, CA, TX and OR for chiros), clinics rely on letters of protection or patient- and attorney-signed lien agreements. These are contract claims, not statutory liens. Since 2023 the trend is to make LOPs **discoverable or disclosable** and to let defendants attack LOP charges: Florida §768.0427 (HB 837, 2023) and Georgia §51-12-1.1 (SB 68, 2025). Texas' 2025 attempt (SB 30) died, but Texas case law (*K&L Auto Crushers*) already opens provider rate discovery.

### Cited Findings
- **Florida §768.0427(3) (HB 837, 2023):** as a condition precedent to claiming medical expenses for LOP treatment, the claimant must disclose a copy of the LOP and all itemized, coded billings. If the receivable was sold to a factoring company or other third party, the claimant must disclose the purchaser and the price. The claimant must also disclose whether the claimant had health coverage, and whether the treatment was referred and by whom. Attorney referrals are disclosable and admissible notwithstanding attorney-client privilege (s. 90.502). "LOP" is defined broadly as any arrangement where a provider treats in exchange for a promise of payment from a judgment or settlement — [Fla. Stat. 768.0427 (flsenate.gov)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/768.0427); [HB 837 final bill analysis](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2023/837/Analyses/h0837z.CJS.PDF); [FindLaw 768.0427](https://codes.findlaw.com/fl/title-xlv-torts/fl-st-sect-768-0427/); [Marshall Dennehey](https://marshalldennehey.com/articles/florida-tort-reform-impact-house-bill-837-health-care-litigation)
- Florida: one firm says most providers must bill health insurance first, with any provider lien covering the remainder — [search summary of Florida practitioner sources](https://www.ftlinjurylaw.com/blog/what-is-a-letter-of-protection-in-a-fort-lauderdale-car-accident-case/) (secondary; verify)
- **Georgia SB 68 (signed April 21, 2025), O.C.G.A. 51-12-1.1:** makes LOP arrangements discoverable, including the LOP agreement, itemized charges with billing codes, and any sale of the receivable to a third party. Sources disagree on whether the trigger is causes of action arising on or after April 21, 2025, or claims brought after April 22, 2025 — [SB 68 signed text](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2025-signed-legislation/sb-68/download); [Hunter Maclean memo](https://www.huntermaclean.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Summary-of-SB-68-Tort-Reform.pdf); [Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2025/georgia-enacts-sweeping-tort-reform-key-takeaways-from-sb-68/)
- **Texas:** SB 30 (2025) would have required LOP disclosure and limited medical-damages evidence. It died when conferees failed to agree before the June 2, 2025 session end — [Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2025/tort-reform-bill-fails-to-pass-in-texas-legislature/); [LegiScan SB30](https://legiscan.com/TX/text/SB30/2025)
- Texas *In re K & L Auto Crushers* (Tex. 2021): where providers treated under attorney LOPs and the defendant served §18.001 counter-affidavits, denying all discovery of providers' billing practices and negotiated rates with insurers was an abuse of discretion. Narrowed requests for rate information are relevant — [Justia opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/texas/supreme-court/2021/19-1022.html); [Cooper & Scully](https://www.cooperscully.com/news-and-resources/articles/in-re-k-l-auto-crushers-llc-an-update-on-north-cypress-and-discovery-of-medical-billing-information)
- **California:** *Pebley v. Santa Clara Organics* (2018): an insured plaintiff who chooses to treat outside the plan (e.g., on lien) is treated as uninsured for economic damages, so full billed lien charges may be recoverable subject to reasonableness evidence — [Pebley (FindLaw)](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-court-of-appeal/1895476.html)
- "Treated on a lien basis" often just means the provider agreed to wait for payment. Whether a statutory lien exists depends on notice and documentation — [Wallace Pierce](https://www.wallacepierce.com/accident-qa-series/what-does-it-mean-that-a-chiropractor-treated-me-on-a-lien-basis-and-do-they-have-to-be-paid-from-my-settlement/)
- Chiropractor reductions on liens are typically negotiated pro rata by the attorney — [adjust.clinic](https://adjust.clinic/whiplash/chiropractors-treating-personal-injury-patients-on-liens-how-does-pro-rata-work/)

### Inferences
- In LOP states, the clinic's enforceable rights come from (a) the patient's signed lien/assignment agreement and (b) the attorney's signed acknowledgment, which usually creates an ethical duty for the attorney to protect funds. The app should store both signed documents and require attorney acknowledgment before treating on LOP.
- Florida and Georgia disclosure rules mean the clinic's billing (CPT-coded itemization), any factoring sale, and referral source become discoverable. The app should keep clean, coded, itemized ledgers and a referral-source field, and record any receivable sale price.
- Arizona and Colorado statutes treat assignments of benefits or lien assignments as part of the statutory scheme (AZ 33-932 "claim, lien or assignment" must be recorded; CO 38-27.5-103 assignee rights).

### Gaps
- No primary source captured on ABA/state bar ethics rules governing attorney obligations under LOPs (many states rely on Rule 1.15 duty to safekeep funds for third-party claimants; NC 2017 FEO 4 is one example). Verify per state.
- Other states with LOP disclosure statutes (e.g., Louisiana 2025 tort reform, South Carolina, Oklahoma) were not researched.

## 3. Interaction with no-fault states (NY, NJ, MI, PA etc.)

### Takeaway
In no-fault states, PIP must generally be billed first and fee schedules apply. NY and NJ expressly bar balance billing the patient above the PIP fee schedule. Michigan's 2019 reform caps provider charges at Medicare-based fee schedules, and a 2024 Court of Appeals decision (*Canty v. Mason*) applied those schedules even in third-party negligence actions. This sharply limits LOP or lien recovery of "excess" charges. LOP/lien treatment generally becomes realistic only for amounts after PIP exhaustion or for non-covered services, and even then fee-schedule limits may follow.

### Cited Findings
- **New York:** Insurance Law §5108(c): a provider may not demand or request any payment in addition to the fee-schedule charges. For services from April 1, 2013, fees above the schedule are not payable (11 NYCRR 65-3.8(g)(1)(ii)) — [NY DFS Reg 83 FAQ](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/apps_and_licensing/property_insurers/faqs_reg83_nofault_schedule); [JT NY Law fee schedule archive](https://jtnylaw.com/legal-encyclopedia/no-fault/fee-schedule/)
- **New Jersey:** N.J.A.C. 11:3-29.6 prohibits balance billing. No person is liable for amounts above the PIP fee schedule (schedule effective for treatment on or after Jan. 4, 2013) — [NJ DOBI fee schedule rule text](https://www.nj.gov/dobi/pipinfo/feeschedruletext_130104.pdf); [Callagy Law](https://callagylaw.com/2015/12/22/reimbursement-under-new-jersey-pip-laws-callagy-law/)
- **Michigan:** MCL 500.3157 ties payable amounts to percentages of Medicare. *Compass Healthcare* rejected a provider's contract-based pursuit of patient "balance bills" because the provider may charge only a "reasonable amount." *Canty v. Mason* (Mich. Ct. App., Oct. 2024) held the 3157 fee schedules apply in third-party negligence actions and mitigation applies — [Speaker Law](https://www.speakerlaw.com/blog/no-faults-one-year-back-rule-applies-to-medical-providers-balance-bills); [Plunkett Cooney on Canty](https://www.plunkettcooney.com/transportation-law-hub/fee-schedule-duty-to-mitigate); [DIFS Bulletin 2021-36](https://www.michigan.gov/-/media/Project/Websites/difs/Bulletins/2021/Bulletin_2021-36-INS.pdf?rev=8bc6945d1f0d49bbb8d59d30d681f487)
- **Statutory liens and workers' comp:** Oklahoma and Washington liens exclude workers' comp claims; Montana excludes property-damage payments — [42 O.S. 46](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-42/section-42-46/); [RCW 60.44](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=60.44&full=true&pdf=true); [MCA 71-3-1114](https://leg.mt.gov/bills/mca/title_0710/chapter_0030/part_0110/section_0140/0710-0030-0110-0140.html)

### Inferences
- In NY/NJ the clinic should treat a no-fault patient's bills as PIP-only up to exhaustion, then possibly LOP/lien for post-exhaustion care. Whether post-exhaustion charges are capped at fee schedule in NY/NJ was not confirmed.
- In Michigan, *Canty* suggests even LOP charges claimed against a tortfeasor are limited to fee-schedule amounts, which reduces LOP value.

### Gaps
- Pennsylvania (75 Pa.C.S. §1797 balance-billing bar and fee schedule), Florida PIP (§627.736, 10k limit), Kentucky, Minnesota, Hawaii, Utah, Kansas, North Dakota no-fault interactions not researched in this pass. Michigan 3157 balance-billing text not quoted. Whether NY/NJ providers may LOP the tort recovery after PIP exhaustion is unverified.

## 4. Interaction with health insurance network contracts (must the provider bill health insurance?)

### Takeaway
A growing number of states condition the lien on billing health insurance or bar liens contrary to network contracts. GA (SB 168) and VA (8.01-27.5) require billing health insurance to keep the lien. AZ voids the lien if an in-network contract does not expressly allow it. CO requires disclosure of insurance payment options. LA's Balance Billing Act gives patients a damages action when an in-network provider liens a settlement instead of billing the insurer (*Anderson v. Ochsner*, La. 2014). Illinois practitioners say providers may still choose the HCSLA lien. Network contracts themselves often require timely claim filing.

### Cited Findings
- Georgia: must submit to health insurance first for the lien to be valid — [OBF Legal](https://obflegal.com/2023/09/georgia-hospital-lien-law-adds-chiropractors-billing-changes/)
- Virginia: under 8.01-27.5, a provider that does not submit bills to health insurance does not get the 8.01-66.2 lien — [C. Pollard Law](https://cpollardlaw.com/personal-injury-attorney-virginia/health-care-provider-health-insurance-medical-liens/); [Allen & Allen](https://www.allenandallen.com/blog/liens-against-personal-injury-recoveries-in-virginia/)
- Arizona: in-network contract must expressly allow a lien or assignment, else the lien is invalid — [A.R.S. 33-931](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/33/00931.htm)
- Colorado HB21-1300: must advise the patient of payment options, including insurance — [HB21-1300](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb21-1300)
- Louisiana *Anderson v. Ochsner* (La. 2014): implied private right of action under the Health Care Consumer Billing and Disclosure Protection Act (La. R.S. 22:1871 et seq.) and an express action under 22:1874(B) where an in-network provider refused to bill the patient's insurer (UnitedHealthcare) and asserted a 9:4752 lien for full undiscounted charges. The Act bars collecting amounts owed by the insurer or above the contracted rate — [Justia opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/louisiana/supreme-court/2014/2013-cc-2970.html); [LASC opinion PDF](https://www.lasc.org/opinions/2014/13CC2970.opn.pdf)
- Illinois: one firm says providers may skip insurance and file an HCSLA lien — [Parker & Parker](https://www.parkerandparkerattorneys.com/blog/hospital-medical-lien-bypass-insurance-illinois/)
- North Carolina: a provider's lien can be barred by its insurance contract, e.g., failure to timely bill the health plan — [Wallace Pierce](https://www.wallacepierce.com/accident-qa-series/can-i-refuse-to-pay-a-medical-lien-that-should-have-been-billed-to-my-health-insurer/)
- Texas: a firm says courts have found refusing to bill insurance may violate Texas law (cases not named) — [Crosley Law](https://crosleylaw.com/blog/insurance-and-hospital-liens-in-texas-personal-injury-cases-what-you-need-to-know/)
- Massachusetts c.111 §§70A–70D reported preempted for self-funded ERISA plans (*Harris v. Harvard Pilgrim*, D. Mass. 1998) — [Scalli Murphy](https://www.scallimurphy.com/erisa-health-plans-and-personal-injury-settlements-in-massachusetts-what-you-need-to-know/)

### Inferences
- For any patient with health insurance where the clinic is **in-network**, the default safe workflow is to bill the health plan (subject to the contract's timely-filing rules) and track the plan's subrogation, not to lien full charges. Out-of-network clinics have more freedom, but GA/VA statutes still require submission to keep the lien.
- App should capture: patient health plan, clinic network status, whether contract permits liens (AZ), date billed to health plan, EOBs.

### Gaps
- No comprehensive 50-state list of "must bill health insurance" rules found. Texas case names not identified. Federal law (No Surprises Act) does not obviously apply to elective chiropractic but was not checked.

## 5. Reduction norms, pro-rata statutes and the common fund doctrine

### Takeaway
Statutory reduction formulas exist in a few states: IL (40% total, 20% per class, plus the one-third limitation), NC (50% of net after fees, pro rata), WA (25% total) and VA (fixed dollar caps). Elsewhere reductions are negotiated, typically pro rata when the settlement is insufficient. Illinois courts have rejected applying the common fund doctrine to further reduce HCSLA liens. Oregon requires insurers to prorate among perfected lienholders.

### Cited Findings
- Illinois: 40%/20% caps; attorney's fees and costs paid first — [770 ILCS 23 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/illinois/chapter-770/act-770-ilcs-23/). No common-fund fee recovery against health care liens under HCSLA — [ISBA Illinois Bar Journal, May 2011](https://www.isba.org/ibj/2011/05/lawpulse/nocommonfundfeerecoveryforhealthcareliens)
- North Carolina: ≤50% of recovery after fees, pro rata — [G.S. 44-50](http://www.ncleg.net/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_44/GS_44-50.html); [Jeffrey Howard Law](https://www.jeffreyhowardlaw.com/medical-liens-and-your-settlement/)
- Washington: 25% cap — [GoldDog Law](https://golddoglaw.com/medical-liens-washington/)
- Virginia: fixed caps; providers often negotiate reductions in practice — [Tysons Trial Law](https://tysonstriallaw.com/virginias-2500-hospital-lien-cap-and-your-mclean-car-accident-settlement/)
- Oregon: insurer prorates available funds among claimants regardless of filing order — [ORS 87.555](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_87.555)
- Colorado: recovery for lien-based treatment capped at usual and customary billed charges — [38-27.5-103](https://law.justia.com/codes/colorado/title-38/liens/article-27-5/section-38-27-5-103/)
- Pro-rata practice for chiropractic liens: attorneys commonly request proportional reductions — [adjust.clinic](https://adjust.clinic/whiplash/chiropractors-treating-personal-injury-patients-on-liens-how-does-pro-rata-work/)

### Inferences
- A common negotiated norm (anecdotal, unsourced here) is a one-third reduction to match the attorney fee, or a pro rata reduction proportional to the client's net. Treat this as negotiation practice, not law.
- The app should compute: gross settlement, attorney fee, costs, net, statutory cap amount (state-specific), all known liens, and a pro rata share, then record the accepted reduction and who approved it.

### Gaps
- Kentucky "pro-rata reduction statute" premise not verified; no source found. Common fund doctrine status in other states (e.g., applied to ERISA/Medicare, varied for provider liens) not researched.

## 6. Practical: fields and steps a case-management app should track per state

### Takeaway
The cited statutes converge on a small set of trackable data points: provider type eligibility, trigger date (first treatment vs discharge), notice recipients, notice method, filing office, filing deadline, cap formula, health-insurance prerequisite, attorney acknowledgment, and release obligation.

### Cited Findings (statute-driven field requirements)
- **Trigger dates:** first-treatment date (AZ 30 days; GA 90 days; WA 20 days) — [33-932](https://law.justia.com/codes/arizona/title-33/section-33-932/); [44-14-471](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-14/article-8/part-8/section-44-14-471/); [RCW 60.44](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=60.44&full=true&pdf=true)
- **Pre-filing notice lead time:** GA notice ≥15 days before filing — [44-14-471](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-44/chapter-14/article-8/part-8/section-44-14-471/)
- **Notice recipients:** patient, patient's attorney, liable party, liability insurer (LA, MA, IL, OK, GA, AZ) — [La. R.S. 9:4753](https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=108041); [42 O.S. 46](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-42/section-42-46/)
- **Notice method:** certified mail RRR or fax with proof (LA); registered/certified (OK, KS, NV, MA); first-class within 5 days of recording (AZ) — sources above
- **Notice contents:** patient name/address, provider name/location, accident date, amount claimed, insurance policies, nature/dates/value of services (KS, OK, MT, LA) — [65-407](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch65/065_004_0007.html); [71-3-1115](https://mca.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0710/chapter_0030/part_0110/section_0150/0710-0030-0110-0150.html)
- **Filing office:** superior court clerk in provider's and patient's counties (GA); county clerk (OK); district court clerk (KS); county recorder (AZ, OR); county auditor (WA) — sources above
- **Hard stop:** most liens must be perfected **before** settlement or payment is disbursed (KS, LA, OK, NV, MA)
- **Enforcement window:** OK suit within 1 year after learning of settlement — [42 O.S. 46](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-42/section-42-46/)
- **Release obligation:** AZ release within 30 days of payment — [Law for Seniors](https://lawforseniors.org/topics/money-and-debt/debt-collection/health-care-provider-liens)
- **Health insurance prerequisite flags:** GA, VA, AZ (in-network), CO disclosure, LA balance billing — section 4 sources
- **LOP disclosure-readiness:** FL/GA require coded itemized bills, LOP copy, factoring sale info, referral source — [768.0427](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2023/768.0427); [SB 68](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2025-signed-legislation/sb-68/download)
- **Itemized statements:** CO lienholders give itemized statements and a final summary (per 2019 bill draft; verify enacted text) — [SB19-217 rev](https://content.leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2019A/bills/2019a_217_rev.pdf)

### Inferences (proposed data model)
- **State rule table (per state):** lien_available_for_chiro (yes/no/unclear), statute_cite, statute_url, last_verified_date, verified_by_attorney (bool), trigger_event (first_treatment | discharge | none), filing_deadline_days, pre_notice_days, notice_recipients[], notice_methods[], filing_office_type, cap_formula (pct_gross | pct_net_after_fees | per_provider_dollar | usual_customary | none), cap_value, attorney_lien_priority (bool), must_bill_health_ins (bool), in_network_lien_restriction (bool), pre_lien_disclosure_required (bool), enforcement_limit_days, release_deadline_days, lop_disclosure_statute (bool), no_fault_state (bool), balance_billing_bar (bool).
- **Per case:** state of accident/venue, date of injury, first-treatment date (auto-compute deadline alerts), patient health plan and clinic network status, PIP/MedPay carrier, claim no., PIP exhaustion date, attorney (firm, contact), LOP/lien agreement signed date (patient), attorney acknowledgment signed date, liability carrier, adjuster, claim no.
- **Per notice event:** recipient type, name/address, method (certified/RRR/fax/first-class/e-mail), tracking no., date sent, date received (green card), document version.
- **Per filing event:** office (county + type), date filed, instrument/recording no., fee, copy-mail date (AZ 5-day rule).
- **Settlement/reduction:** settlement date, gross, fees, costs, net, statutory cap computed, other liens, reduction requested, reduction accepted, approver, payment received date, lien release date (AZ 30-day).
- **Compliance flags:** "perfect before disbursement" warning; health-insurance-billing prerequisite warning; factoring sale disclosure (FL/GA); referral source recorded.

### Gaps
- The proposed fields are derived from the statutes sampled. They must be extended once the remaining ~30 states are verified. No official state chiropractic association multi-state guide was located; the Colorado Chiropractic Association lien materials are the only association source found.
