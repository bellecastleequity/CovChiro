## Pennsylvania (MVFRL, 75 Pa.C.S. Ch. 17; 31 Pa. Code Ch. 69)

### Takeaway
PA is a "choice" state. First-party medical benefits are a $5,000 minimum with optional higher tiers. Provider charges are capped at 110% of Medicare (Act 6, §1797(a)) and balance billing is banned. Insurers pay within 30 days of reasonable proof, or 12%/yr interest accrues (§1716). Disputed treatment goes to Peer Review Organization (PRO) review under strict 90/30/30-day clocks (31 Pa. Code §69.52). Whether the patient can sue for pain and suffering depends on their full tort vs. limited tort election.

### Cited Findings
**Benefit limits / options**
- Above the $5,000 minimum, insurers commonly offer first-party medical tiers of $10,000, $25,000, $50,000 or $100,000 — [Simon & Simon / Leeson Law / Lebovitz explainers via search](https://www.leeson-law.com/limited-tort-vs-full-tort-pennsylvania/). (The $5,000 minimum under §1711 is the parent's premise and is widely reported. The exact §1712 tier list was not confirmed from statute text.)

**Act 6 fee schedule / balance billing (§1797(a); 31 Pa. Code Ch. 69 Subch. A)**
- §1797 (Act 6) sets reimbursable amounts for motor-vehicle-accident treatment at 110% of what Medicare would pay for the same service — [Simon & Simon, "Excess Medical Bills"](https://www.gosimon.com/excess-medical-bills/).
- The regulations say insurers "shall apply the Medicare payment limitations of Act 6" to provider services under bodily injury liability, UM/UIM, first-party medical and extraordinary medical benefits coverages. "Medicare payment" = 110% of the Medicare reimbursement allowance, or a different allowance under §69.12(b) — [31 Pa. Code Ch. 69 Subch. A](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=/secure/pacode/data/031/chapter69/subchapAtoc.html&d=reduce).
- Do not confuse this with PA **workers' comp**, which pays chiropractic CMT codes 98940-98943 at 113% of Medicare. That is a separate WC rule (34 Pa. Code Ch. 127) — [34 Pa. Code Ch. 127 Subch. B](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=%2Fsecure%2Fpacode%2Fdata%2F034%2Fchapter127%2FsubchapBtoc.html&d=reduce).
- Balance billing is also unlawful under the 1990 Health Care Practitioners Medicare Fee Control Act, whose definition of practitioner includes chiropractors — [Act 1990-81](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/WU01/LI/LI/US/HTM/1990/0/0081..HTM).
- The insured may not be billed during the peer review process — [31 Pa. Code §69.52 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/pennsylvania/31-Pa-Code-SS-69-52).

**Payment deadline and interest (§1716)**
- Benefits are overdue if not paid within 30 days after the insurer receives reasonable proof of the amount. Overdue benefits bear interest at 12% per annum from the date due. If proof covers only part of the claim, that part is overdue 30 days after the proof arrives — [Margolis Edelstein MVFRL compilation (2024)](https://www.margolisedelstein.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/PA-MVFRL-2024.pdf); [Schappell v. Motorists Mutual](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/pa-supreme-court/1237896.html).
- Attorney fees: §1716 requires a reasonable fee when the insurer acted unreasonably in refusing to pay benefits when due. §1798(b) uses a similar "no reasonable foundation" standard. §1798(a) bars fees for contingent-fee representation on a first-party claim — [Burns White](https://burnswhite.com/despite-attorney-fee-award-six-times-greater-than-contract-claim-superior-court-affirms/); [Margolis Edelstein](https://www.margolisedelstein.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/PA-MVFRL-2024.pdf).
- A federal court indicated a provider may have a private right of action under §1716 for unpaid bills plus 12% interest. Whether that is settled law was not established — [E.D. Pa. opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-paed-2_10-cv-07133/pdf/USCOURTS-paed-2_10-cv-07133-0.pdf).

**PRO peer review (§1797(b); 31 Pa. Code §69.52)**
- The insurer must refer a bill to a PRO within **90 days** of receiving sufficient documentation. Bills not referred must be paid within **30 days** of that documentation. If the referral comes after day 30 and on or before day 90, the bill must be paid (sources differ on wording) — [31 Pa. Code §69.52 (Cornell)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/pennsylvania/31-Pa-Code-SS-69-52); [PA Code & Bulletin §69.52](https://www.pacodeandbulletin.gov/Display/pacode?file=%2Fsecure%2Fpacode%2Fdata%2F031%2Fchapter69%2Fs69.52.html&d=reduce).
- The PRO's initial determination is due **30 days** after it receives the requested information. That period can be tolled up to **20 days** if critical additional information is submitted or requested — [same](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/pennsylvania/31-Pa-Code-SS-69-52).
- Any party may request **reconsideration within 30 days** of the initial determination. A different licensed practitioner must perform the reconsideration — [same](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/pennsylvania/31-Pa-Code-SS-69-52).
- Under §1797(b), if the PRO finds the treatment reasonable and necessary, the insurer pays the outstanding balance plus 12% interest. A federal court held that §1716/§1798 attorney fees are not recoverable when the PRO process is used — [E.D. Pa. opinion summary](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-paed-2_10-cv-07133/pdf/USCOURTS-paed-2_10-cv-07133-0.pdf).
- Chiropractic case law: *Turnpaugh Chiropractic Health & Wellness v. Erie*, 2023 PA Super 99, held that sending a chiropractor's bills to peer review is not a "refusal to pay" that triggers MVFRL attorney fees — [Superior Court opinion](https://www.pacourts.us/assets/opinions/Superior/out/J-A23032-22o%20-%20105564280227182474.pdf?cb=1); [Bordas & Bordas summary](https://www.bordaslaw.com/blog-posts/pennsylvania-superior-court-denies-claim-for-attorneys-fees-in-motor-vehicle-provider-peer-review-context/).

**Limited vs full tort / serious injury**
- "Serious injury" (§1702) = personal injury resulting in death, serious impairment of body function, or permanent serious disfigurement. "Serious impairment" is not defined by statute. *Washington v. Baxter* (Pa. 1998) adopted a case-law standard taken from Michigan's *DiFranco* decision — [Lebovitz Law](https://lebovitzlaw.com/personal-injury-lawyer-pittsburgh/serious-injury-exception-limited-tort-pennsylvania/); [Simon & Simon](https://www.gosimon.com/limited-tort/serious-impairment/); [Wikipedia overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_tort_and_limited_tort_automobile_insurance).
- §1705(d) exceptions let a limited-tort insured sue as if they had full tort. One confirmed exception: the at-fault driver was convicted of DUI, or accepted ARD, for the crash — [Brandon J. Broderick (2026)](https://www.brandonjbroderick.com/pennsylvania/limited-tort-vs-full-tort-pennsylvania-why-your-insurance-choice-still-matters-2026). (Search did not return the full exception list.)
- The tort election does not affect first-party medical benefits. Limited tort restricts only non-economic (pain and suffering) recovery — [Leeson Law](https://www.leeson-law.com/limited-tort-vs-full-tort-pennsylvania/).

### Inferences
- App config for PA: a per-claim `first_party_medical_limit` (default $5,000; selectable tiers); a fee cap engine at 110% of the Medicare PA locality allowance, with any write-off above the cap non-collectible from the patient; a `tort_option` field (full/limited) plus a serious-injury flag and an exceptions checklist; and a PRO workflow with timers (90-day referral window, 30-day pay, 30-day PRO decision + 20-day toll, 30-day reconsideration). The Florida "80% reimbursement" logic should be replaced, since PA pays 100% of the allowed amount up to the benefit limit.
- Once first-party benefits are exhausted, excess medical bills generally go to the patient's health insurance or the BI claim. Act 6 limits still apply to BI and UM/UIM coverage, per the regulation quoted above, which limits LOP-style "full charge" recovery.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the exact §1712 optional tier amounts, the §1715 extraordinary medical benefits ($100k–$1.1M), or the full §1705(d) exception list (believed to include out-of-state vehicles, uninsured at-fault drivers, and injury in a commercial vehicle) from primary text.
- Required billing forms: the requirement to submit HCFA/CMS-1500 or UB-04 forms with supporting records (believed to be 31 Pa. Code §69.22 / §69.43) was not confirmed by search. Treat it as needing verification.
- I did not research PA's 4-year statute of limitations for first-party claims (§1721) or the 2-year tort SOL.
- Recent reforms 2019–2026: I found no reform to the first-party limits or the Act 6 percentage. Status not confirmed.

## Massachusetts (M.G.L. c.90 §§34A, 34M; c.231 §6D; 211 CMR 38.00)

### Takeaway
Mandatory $8,000 PIP covers medical, 75% of lost wages and replacement services combined. When the patient has private health insurance, PIP pays only the first $2,000 of medical. Health insurance is then primary, and PIP picks up health-plan gaps (deductibles, copays, non-covered services) up to the remaining $6,000. Benefits unpaid after 30 days let the unpaid party (including a provider) sue the insurer in contract and recover costs and attorney fees. The tort threshold is >$2,000 of reasonable and necessary medical expenses or a listed injury. There is no statutory PIP fee schedule like PA or HI.

### Cited Findings
- PIP = $8,000 per person under c.90 §§34A/34M. It did **not** change when MA raised its other minimum limits effective July 1, 2025 — [VettedRisk guide](https://vettedrisk.com/guides/massachusetts-pip-personal-injury-protection/); [masspiplawyer.com](https://masspiplawyer.com/key-pip-billing-rules-for-ma-healthcare-providers/).
- The $8,000 is a single limit covering medical, up to 75% of lost wages, and replacement services — [Diller Law](https://www.dillerlaw.com/massachusetss-auto-insurance.html); [VettedRisk](https://vettedrisk.com/guides/massachusetts-pip-personal-injury-protection/).
- Coordination: by statute, PIP will not pay more than $2,000 of medical expenses to the extent they are or will be paid by a health/sickness policy or health-care plan. The health plan is then primary, and unpaid deductibles, copays and non-covered amounts can go back to PIP up to an additional $6,000. 211 CMR 38.05 is cited as making PIP primary for the first $2,000 — [MFC Law](https://mfclaw.com/blogs/do-i-need-pip-if-i-have-health-insurance-massachusetts/); [mass.gov Coordination of Benefits](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/coordination-of-benefits); [Labovick](https://www.labovick.com/blog/how-does-pip-work-in-massachusetts/).
- Patients with no private health insurance, or covered by Medicare, MassHealth or (per some sources) ERISA plans, may get PIP up to the full $8,000. Sources are inconsistent on ERISA — [masspiplawyer.com](https://masspiplawyer.com/key-pip-billing-rules-for-ma-healthcare-providers/); [Labovick](https://www.labovick.com/blog/how-does-pip-work-in-massachusetts/).
- Older official jury instruction text describes "$2,000 per person" plus "an additional $6,000 for unpaid bills after health insurance" (Nov 2021). This is the same structure, stated differently — [mass.gov model jury instruction 8.00 PIP](https://www.mass.gov/doc/800-personal-injury-protection-pip/download).
- Covered expenses = reasonable expenses **incurred within two years** of the accident for necessary medical, surgical, x-ray and dental services, including chiropractic — [mass.gov §34M](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/mass-general-laws-c90-ss-34m); [Sugarman](https://www.sugarman.com/news-and-blog/personal-injury-lawyers-car-insurance-what-is-the-difference-between-personal-injury-protection-and-medical-payment-coverage-in-massachusetts/).
- **30-day rule / provider standing:** "In any case where benefits due and payable remain unpaid for more than thirty days, any unpaid party shall be deemed a party to a contract with the insurer responsible for payment." The prevailing party recovers costs and reasonable attorney fees. The model jury instruction says a provider may sue when the insurer does not pay in full within 30 days of receiving the bills **and medical records** — [mass.gov §34M](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/mass-general-laws-c90-ss-34m); [mass.gov jury instruction](https://www.mass.gov/doc/800-personal-injury-protection-pip/download).
- **IME:** under §34M the insurer may require the injured person to submit to physical examinations by physicians it selects "as often as may be reasonably required." Insurers use IMEs to cut off chiropractic treatment as not reasonable or necessary — [mass.gov §34M](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/mass-general-laws-c90-ss-34m); [masspiplawyer: insurer stall tactics](https://masspiplawyer.com/how-insurers-stall-pip-payments-and-what-providers-can-do/).
- Chiropractic PIP litigation by providers against carriers is common, e.g. *Metropolitan Prop. & Cas. v. Savin Hill Family Chiropractic* — [CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/7326691/metropolitan-property-casualty-insurance-co-v-savin-hill-family/?q=cites%3A%286446056%29).
- PIP billing deadline: one provider-side firm says MA law sets no rigid billing deadline in days, but practice and courts expect submission within 2 years. Another source says the PIP claim must be submitted within 2 years of the accident. The two conflict and neither is verified — [masspiplawyer.com](https://masspiplawyer.com/key-pip-billing-rules-for-ma-healthcare-providers/); [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/how-pip-works-in-massachusetts-coverage-and-filing/).
- **Tort threshold (c.231 §6D):** pain and suffering is recoverable only if reasonable and necessary medical expenses exceed $2,000, or the injury involves death, loss of a body member, permanent and serious disfigurement, loss of sight or hearing, or a fracture. The threshold counts the full reasonable billed amount, not what PIP paid. Chiropractic and PT count toward it, though adjusters challenge chiropractic visit counts — [MFC Law](https://mfclaw.com/blogs/massachusetts-tort-threshold-rules-explained/); [Ballin Law](https://www.ballinlaw.com/what-is-the-2000-threshold-to-sue-after-a-boston-truck-crash/); [Injury Claim Coach](https://www.injuryclaimcoach.com/questions/2000-threshold-in-massachusetts.html).
- **Liens:** the c.111 §70A lien belongs to hospitals, HMOs and hospital/medical/dental service corporations. Individual physicians and other practitioners acting in their own capacity are not lien holders under §70A. The attorney's lien has priority. The lien is perfected only by written notice under §70B (certified mail, before settlement or judgment, to the patient, attorney, liable party and insurer). Notice requirements are ERISA-preempted for self-funded plans — [Suffolk Univ. paper](https://dc.suffolk.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1205&context=suls-faculty); [M.G.L. c.111 §70A](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXVI/Chapter111/Section70A); [Margolis Bloom](https://margolisbloom.com/planning-for-life/massachusetts-bankruptcy-case-highlights-medical-lien-law/); [Ballin Law on §70B](https://www.ballinlaw.com/what-is-a-masshealth-lien-on-a-foxborough-car-accident-settlement/).
- MedPay is an optional coverage separate from PIP — [Sugarman](https://www.sugarman.com/news-and-blog/personal-injury-lawyers-car-insurance-what-is-the-difference-between-personal-injury-protection-and-medical-payment-coverage-in-massachusetts/).

### Inferences
- App config for MA needs a `has_private_health_insurance` flag, which drives a two-phase payer sequence. Phase 1: PIP primary for medical up to $2,000. Phase 2: bill the health plan, then send its EOB balances (deductibles, copays, denials) back to PIP up to $8,000 total, minus any wage or replacement payments already made from the same $8,000 pool. Without private health insurance, PIP is primary up to $8,000.
- Track a "bills + records received" date per bill and a 30-day overdue timer that opens the provider's statutory suit right with fee shifting. Track a 2-year incurred-expense window from the date of loss.
- The tort threshold tracker should sum billed (not paid) reasonable medical charges against $2,000, plus a listed-injury checklist including fracture.
- A chiropractor is not a statutory §70A lien holder. LOPs and attorney-protection letters are contractual only. (Common practice, but no source found describing it specifically.)

### Gaps
- I could not verify the PIP application form name or the notice deadline. Cited sources say only "as soon as possible", and there is a 2-year vs. 3-year conflict. I also did not verify 211 CMR 38.00 details such as the 10-day initial-response rule (mentioned in a snippet but unconfirmed), PIP deductible options, or the insurer's denial-notice requirements under §34A.
- No source confirmed whether health insurance "must" pay after $2,000 when a chiropractor is out-of-network or the plan excludes chiropractic. The mechanism is that PIP covers what health insurance does not, up to $8,000.
- Recent reforms: July 1, 2025 minimum limit increases (PIP unchanged). I did not confirm the new BI figures.

## Hawaii (HRS Ch. 431:10C; HAR Title 16 Ch. 23)

### Takeaway
Hawaii requires $10,000 minimum PIP. Under HRS §431:10C-308.5, provider **charges and frequency of treatment** are capped at the Hawaii workers' compensation supplemental medical fee schedule (except emergency services within 72 hours of the accident). That schedule is 110% of Hawaii Medicare RBRVS participating fees, with Exhibit A controlling where both list a code. HAR Ch. 23 (motor vehicle insurance rules) allows the first 15 treatments in 60 days without authorization; beyond that, a treatment plan is needed. The tort threshold is fixed at $5,000 of PIP medical-rehabilitative benefits; it is no longer set annually. Insurers must deny within 30 days in writing with reasons, and overdue benefits bear 1.5%/month interest.

### Cited Findings
- PIP minimum $10,000 per person (HRS §431:10C-103.5, -304). A Jan 1, 2026 change raised **liability** minimums to $40,000/$80,000. PIP stays at $10,000 — [FastAutoQuote 2026](https://fastautoquote.org/requirements/hawaii); [Island Insurance](https://www.islandinsurance.com/article/hawaiis-personal-injury-protection-pip-benefits-explained). (Secondary sources; verify the 2026 liability change with the DCCA.)
- §431:10C-308.5: "The charges and frequency of treatment for services specified in section 431:10C-103.5(a), except for emergency services provided within seventy-two hours following a motor vehicle accident resulting in injury, shall not exceed the charges and frequency of treatment permissible under the workers' compensation supplemental medical fee schedule." The commissioner may adopt PIP fee and frequency rules that prevail over the WC schedule — [Justia HRS §431:10C-308.5 (2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/hawaii/title-24/chapter-431/section-431-10c-308-5/); [FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/hi/division-2-business/hi-rev-st-sect-431-10c-308-5/).
- The WC medical fee schedule = Medicare RBRVS applicable to Hawaii + Exhibit A (HAR 12-15). Charges may not exceed 110% of Medicare participating fees. Where a code appears in both, Exhibit A's maximum controls — [HAR §16-23-115 (Cornell)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/hawaii/Haw-Code-R-SS-16-23-115); [HAR §12-15-90 (Cornell)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/hawaii/Haw-Code-R-SS-12-15-90).
- Exhibit A lists chiropractic CMT codes (e.g. 98940). The 2018 Exhibit A fee for 98940 was $30.19, and current figures need checking — [2018 Exhibit A](https://labor.hawaii.gov/dcd/files/2017/12/FINAL-2018-Exhibit-A.pdf).
- **Treatment frequency / plans (HAR Ch. 23, motor vehicle insurance):** authorization is not required for the initial 15 treatments during the first 60 calendar days (an exception exists; its text was truncated in the excerpt). Beyond that, a treatment plan is required. The provider is paid for the initial treatment plan per the Exhibit A fee, but not for later plans unless the diagnosis, condition or prognosis has changed significantly. The frequency guidelines are "a guideline to improve health care provider accountability", not a prescription for care. Chiropractic payments "shall not be reduced or revised by an insurer or its agent(s)" (context truncated) and must stay within scope of practice under HRS chs. 431 and 442 — [HAR Ch. 23 (DCCA Ins. Div.)](https://files.hawaii.gov/dcca/ins/har/har_23-c.pdf); [HAR Ch. 23 (OAH copy)](https://files.hawaii.gov/dcca/oah/forms/mvi/har-chapter-23-web.pdf).
- The parallel WC rule, HAR §12-15-32: no authorization for the first 15 treatments in 60 days. Additional treatment needs a treatment plan sent to the payer, covering up to 120 days and no more than 15 treatments per plan — [HAR Title 12 Ch. 15](https://labor.hawaii.gov/dcd/files/2012/11/MFS-RULES-WEB.pdf); [WorkersCompensation.com](https://www.workerscompensation.com/daily-headlines/when-is-a-treatment-plan-not-required-for-cervical-and-lumbar-spinal-injuries-in-hawaii/).
- **Payment / interest / denial (§431:10C-304):** benefits unpaid 30 days after the insurer receives reasonable proof of the fact and amount, and demand for payment, bear interest at 1.5% per month (confirmed in 2009 text; the current text needs confirmation). The interest applies when payment is delayed by a billing dispute. If the insurer denies a claim in whole or part, it must notify the claimant in writing within 30 days with reasons — [Justia §431:10C-304 (2025)](https://law.justia.com/codes/hawaii/title-24/chapter-431/section-431-10c-304/); [Justia 2009 text](https://law.justia.com/codes/hawaii/2009/volume-09/title-24/chapter-431/hrs-0431-0010c-0304-htm/).
- **Disputes:** PIP denials can be contested by administrative hearing before the DCCA Office of Administrative Hearings (OAH maintains a "Motor Vehicle Insurance" forms page and the HAR Ch. 23 copy). Provider–insurer disputes have reached the Hawaii courts, e.g. *Hawaii Providers Network v. AIG Hawaii* — [OAH HAR Ch. 23](https://files.hawaii.gov/dcca/oah/forms/mvi/har-chapter-23-web.pdf); [Hawaii Providers Network v. AIG](http://oaoa.hawaii.gov/jud/23790.htm).
- **Tort threshold (§431:10C-306 / -308):** the legislature repealed the annually set floating "medical-rehabilitative limit" and fixed the tort threshold at $5,000 of PIP medical-rehabilitative benefits. Alternatives are death or listed serious or permanent injuries — [Hawaii Supreme Court opinion (2020)](https://www.courts.state.hi.us/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/SCWC-12-0001090ada.pdf); [Onecle §431:10C-306](https://law.onecle.com/hawaii/title-24/431-10c-306.html); [RH Law HI](https://rhlawhi.com/personal-injury/what-you-should-know-about-hawai%CA%BBis-no-fault-insurance-laws/).
- One clinic marketing site claims "approximately 30 chiropractic treatments" are allowed. This conflicts with the 15-in-60 rule and is unreliable — [search result summary; not cited as fact].

### Inferences
- App config for HI: a fee cap engine using the HI WC schedule (Exhibit A first, else 110% of HI Medicare RBRVS), with an emergency-within-72-hours exemption flag. Add a visit counter that warns at treatment 15 or day 60 from first treatment and requires a treatment-plan record (≤15 treatments / 120 days per plan, per the WC analog) before further billing. Bill the initial treatment plan as a billable item, but block later plans unless a "significant change" is documented. Track a 30-day denial/payment timer with 1.5%/month interest. Run a tort threshold tracker against $5,000 of PIP medical-rehab benefits **paid or accrued**, not billed charges (unlike MA).
- Because charges are capped by statute, balance billing above the schedule is likely improper. However, an explicit patient-billing prohibition was not located in the excerpts (see Gaps).

### Gaps

- Not confirmed: the deadline to request an OAH hearing after denial (sources gave 60 vs. 20 days, possibly mixed up with WC rules), and whether arbitration (§431:10C-213) is an option.
- Not confirmed: the PIP claim notice/limitations period (believed to be 2 years from the last PIP payment or the accident, HRS §431:10C-315) and the required forms (PIP claim/application, treatment plan form).
- The full exception text in HAR 16-23's 15-treatment rule and the current-year Exhibit A chiropractic dollar amounts were not retrieved.
- Recent reforms: 2026 liability minimum increase only (secondary sources). No PIP-limit change found.
